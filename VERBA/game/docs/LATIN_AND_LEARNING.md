# Latin policy for VERBA

Latin is not decorative nonsense in this game. Each entry includes its lemma and grammatical form in `data/lexicon.json`. Names (`ignis`, `pluvia`, `lux`, `ventus`, `aqua`, `nox`, `flamma`, `gelu`) are **nouns invoked as names of phenomena**; they are not commands. The verbs (`cresce`, `veni`, `dormi`, `fuge`, `siste`, `muta`, `arde`, `surge`) are singular present active imperatives.

The meaning of a Latin word is grounded in real language. Its **magical outcome is fictional**, and the magical semantics need not always match colloquial usage: calling `ignis` produces fire. Uncertainty (wild magic) affects the consequences, not whether the Latin word is a real form.

Future grammar must recognize declensions, number, and case using explicitly validated paradigms. For example `arborem move` (accusative singular object + singular imperative) versus `arbores, crescite` (vocative plural address + plural imperative) may become different patterns. No mere English-to-Latin word substitution engine or generative guesses are acceptable.

**Pedagogy:** players acquire words by reading folios in an actual book; recall is faster than consulting. All successful casts update the record. A known valid word typed before collecting its leaf is still usable. Next iteration: differentiated mastery tracking per lemma and inflected form, contextual Latin hints in-world, optional spaced repetition that does not interrupt play.
